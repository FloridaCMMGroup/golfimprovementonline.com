The two camera angles every coach needs

Face-on. Stand the phone directly across from your chest, facing you, like you're looking in a mirror. This view shows ball position, posture, weight shift, and how your arms and body move together.

Down-the-line. Put the phone behind you, pointed at your target, in line with your hands, not the ball. This view shows your swing path, the club's position at the top, and whether you're actually aimed where you think you are.

Send both. One angle alone hides half the story. It does!

Phone setup that works

  • Height: about waist high. A tripod works, and so does a stack of balls on your golf bag.
  • Distance: far enough back that your whole body and the full club fit in the frame at the top of your swing, usually 10 to 15 feet.
  • Orientation: sideways (landscape) for face-on. Upright (portrait) for down-the-line, if that keeps the whole club in view.
  • Slow motion: use it if your phone has it. More frames per second means a clearer look at impact.
  • Light: record with the sun behind the camera, not behind you, so you're not a silhouette.

Small things that make a big difference

Wear fitted clothing. Your coach needs to see your body move. A baggy jacket hides your hips completely.

Lay down an alignment stick along your target line. It shows your coach your aim in a single glance.

Record your normal swing, not your best range swing. Your coach needs to see what you actually do on the course.

Add a short note: your goal, your common miss and how often you play.

Grab a 7-iron and your phone. Record three swings face-on and three down-the-line using the setup above. Then watch them back once.
